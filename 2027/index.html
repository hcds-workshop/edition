Workshop Overview

Abstract

Modern computing infrastructures are becoming increasingly heterogeneous, integrating CPUs, GPUs, NPUs, FPGAs, and specialized accelerators, alongside diverse memory technologies, to meet the growing demands of emerging applications. However, efficiently composing and managing these heterogeneous resources remains challenging, requiring new approaches to resource allocation, scheduling, communication, and programming. At the same time, technologies such as Compute Express Link (CXL) enable new opportunities for heterogeneous memory sharing, pooling, and disaggregation, increasing the flexibility and complexity of computing systems.
Heterogeneity extends beyond hardware to the workloads running on these systems. Emerging applications, including large language models (LLMs), multimodal AI, and agentic systems, exhibit diverse and dynamically changing compute, memory, and communication requirements. Alongside HPC and data-intensive applications, these workloads motivate new approaches to efficiently mapping and orchestrating heterogeneous resources.
HCDS 2027 invites original research on architectures, systems software, programming abstractions, and evaluation methodologies for efficient, scalable, and reliable heterogeneous composable and disaggregated systems.

Call for Papers

Heterogeneous and Composable Disaggregated Systems (HCDS), provide a system design approach for reducing the imbalance between workloads resource requirements and the static availability of resources in a computing system, while making room for novel distributed system approaches in processes communication and data exchange.
The HCDS workshop aims at exploring the novel research ideas around composable disaggregated systems and their integration with operating systems and software runtimes to maximize the benefit perceived from user workloads.

Areas of interest include, but are not limited to:

System Software and Programming Models / Tools

  • Operating-system designs for disaggregated resources: memory profiling, allocation, tiering, page migration, and data placement
  • Control planes, runtime systems, and virtualization for composable compute, memory, and accelerator resources
  • Programming models, interfaces, and profiling/analysis tools for heterogeneous resource composition
  • Performance isolation, resource protection, QoS/SLO guarantees, and AI-assisted or agentic infrastructure management

Applications and Use Cases

  • Composable infrastructure for LLM inference, serving, training, and fine-tuning, including disaggregated inference pipelines
  • Agentic and multi-agent services on composable infrastructure, including shared-context placement, inter-agent communication, elastic resource allocation, and performance isolation across distributed resources
  • KV-cache placement and movement, CXL memory sharing, accelerator pooling, and co-design of AI models with system disaggregation
  • Data-intensive analytics, in-memory databases, graph processing, streaming pipelines, scientific applications, and HPC workloads

Modeling and Evaluation

  • Performance, scalability, energy efficiency, and reliability characterization of heterogeneous composable systems
  • Workload and infrastructure characterization for LLMs, agentic services, and heterogeneous execution environments
  • Analysis of interference, remote-resource overhead, service guarantees, and total cost of ownership
  • Models and evaluation methods for resource-management policies, including intelligent and adaptive provisioning

Hardware and Prototyping

  • Heterogeneous CPU/GPU/NPU architectures, composable accelerators, CXL-based systems, and high-speed interconnects supporting resource disaggregation and cross-device communication
  • Memory pooling, memory disaggregation, and heterogeneous accelerator sharing and composition
  • Hardware–software interfaces for disaggregated resource discovery, access, isolation, and management
  • System prototypes and proof-of-concept implementations demonstrating composability in practical deployments

Submission Instructions

Each manuscript will be evaluated with a double-blind review process. Authors must omit at submission time their names or any information that can disclose their identity. Manuscripts length must be 4-6 pages including figures and tables but excluding references. All manuscripts must use the sigplan ACM conference style. It is also expected that all accepted papers will be presented at the workshop by one of the authors. Papers accepted to the workshop will have the chance to be published on the ACM Digital Library following the new ACM open access publishing model.

We offer the possibility to opt-out from publishing submitted work to the ACM proceedings. This applies for instance to authors wishing to re-submit an extended version to another venue that does not allow the work to have appeared in official proceedings or, the authors are not part of the ACM open network and do not have the funds for supporting the proceedings processing costs. In such case, if your manuscript is accepted, it will only be published on the workshop website. If you are wishing to do so, send the organizers an email right after submitting your manuscript.
